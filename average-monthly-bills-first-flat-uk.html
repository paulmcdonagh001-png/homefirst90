FIRST-FLAT MONEY

What will the monthly bills for your first flat actually cost?

There is no useful single “average” for everyone. The better answer is to build the monthly total from the property, who lives there and the bills you will actually pay.

Calculate my monthly bills →

The bills most first-time movers need to plan for

  • Rent or mortgage — usually the largest monthly cost, so enter the real figure rather than an average.
  • Council Tax — depends on the property band and local authority. A qualifying single adult may be able to claim a discount.
  • Gas and electricity — property size, heating type and usage can move this significantly.
  • Water — metered and unmetered homes work differently, so replace any planning estimate with the real account figure once known.
  • Broadband and mobile — especially important if you work from home.
  • Contents insurance — often small beside rent, but easy to forget.
  • TV licence and subscriptions — only include what applies to your household.
Best approach: start with sensible planning figures, then replace each one with the actual quote or bill as moving day gets closer.

Why your first-month number can be misleading

The first month can include setup costs that are not normal recurring bills: delivery charges, small household purchases, temporary broadband solutions and extra travel. Keep your moving-out cash separate from your ongoing monthly household total.

Do not forget the “small” monthly costs

A flat can look affordable when you only compare rent with income. Add together council tax, utilities, broadband, insurance and subscriptions and the picture becomes much more realistic. HomeFirst90 keeps the monthly estimate alongside your setup cost so you can see both sides of the move.
