AFTER THE KEYS

Your first 48 hours should be about control, not finishing the whole house.

Get the property documented, safe, usable and connected first. The less urgent jobs can move into the following days and weeks.

What to do first

  1. Take meter readings and photographs. Record the starting point before normal usage begins.
  2. Photograph the condition of the property. For renters, capture any inventory or condition issues promptly; owners may also want a baseline for maintenance.
  3. Find the main controls. Locate the stopcock, consumer unit/fuse box and heating controls.
  4. Check alarms and basic security. Confirm smoke alarms work, check doors/windows and account for the keys.
  5. Make one bedroom, the bathroom and a basic kitchen usable. You do not need every room finished.
  6. Confirm essential utilities and connectivity. Make sure power, water and the internet plan are under control.
  7. Write down what is actually missing. Do not automatically buy everything that was on the pre-move wish list.
What can wait? Decorative purchases, perfect storage systems and non-urgent furniture usually become easier decisions after you have lived in the home for a few days.

Why giant moving checklists stop working after the move

Once the keys arrive, priorities change quickly. A job that mattered before the move may disappear; a new repair, bill or missing item may suddenly become urgent. That is why HomeFirst90 Complete is organised around “what should I do next?” rather than showing the entire 90-day list at once.

What happens after 48 hours?

Week one is usually about council tax, utilities, broadband, immediate admin and getting the home functioning. Days 8–30 are where estimated costs should start being replaced with real bills and your next purchases can be made from the money actually left.
